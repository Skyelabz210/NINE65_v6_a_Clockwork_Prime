# FHE Libraries and Quantum Computing Services: Pricing Models for Academic Institutions

## FHE Libraries

### Microsoft SEAL (Somewhat Encrypted Arithmetic Library)
- **Licensing**: MIT License - completely open source
- **Academic Use**: Free to use and modify for both academic and commercial projects
- **Pricing Model**: No cost - completely free
- **Access**: Available on GitHub under MIT license

### OpenFHE
- **Licensing**: BSD 2-Clause License - open source and commercial-friendly
- **Academic Use**: Free to use for academic and commercial purposes
- **Pricing Model**: No cost - completely free
- **Access**: Available on GitHub under BSD 2-Clause license
- **Note**: The BSD license was specifically chosen to be open and commercial-friendly

### PALISADE
- **Licensing**: BSD 2-Clause License - open source and commercial-friendly
- **Academic Use**: Free to use for academic and commercial purposes
- **Pricing Model**: No cost - completely free
- **Note**: OpenFHE was developed incorporating design ideas from PALISADE

## Quantum Computing Services

### IBM Quantum
- **General Pricing**: $0.01 to $1 per second per qubit for cloud-based services
- **Academic Access**: 
  - Part of IBM Quantum Network (300+ members including academic institutions)
  - No specific academic pricing mentioned on the main network page
  - Qualifying access plans for contracts worth $250,000+ may include network membership
  - Free access to basic systems for learning and development (likely includes academic institutions)
  - Premium enterprise plans with guaranteed uptime, priority queue access, and dedicated support

### Google Quantum
- **General Pricing**: $0.01 to $1 per second per qubit for cloud-based services
- **Academic Access**:
  - Major partnerships with universities (e.g., University of Chicago and University of Tokyo partnership with $100M commitment over 10 years)
  - Access to advanced quantum processors (up to 72 superconducting qubits)
  - Google Cloud credits for students and faculty
  - Faculty research grants for quantum research
  - Graduate and undergraduate research funding
  - No direct commercial pricing model for academic access - primarily through partnerships and grants

### Amazon Braket
- **General Pricing**: Up to $1,000 per hour for high-fidelity simulations; $0.30 per task plus $0.10 per shot for IonQ's 11-qubit system
- **Academic Access**:
  - AWS Cloud Credit for Research Program specifically supports university researchers
  - Provides funded credits to start research before securing grants
  - Available to full-time faculty, research staff, and graduate/PhD students
  - 1 free hour of simulation time per month through AWS Free Tier
  - Partnerships with research institutions (e.g., National Science Foundation groups, Italian National Institute for Nuclear Physics, etc.)

## Academic Pricing Models Summary

### FHE Libraries
- All major FHE libraries (Microsoft SEAL, OpenFHE, PALISADE) follow an open-source model
- No cost for academic institutions
- MIT and BSD licenses allow for free use in academic research
- No differentiation between academic and commercial use

### Quantum Computing Services
- Cloud-based pay-per-use models with academic-specific programs
- Academic partnerships often include:
  - Free or subsidized access to quantum hardware
  - Cloud credits for supporting computations
  - Research grants and funding
  - Educational and workforce development programs
- Direct pricing is typically available but academic institutions often access services through special programs

## Key Trends
1. FHE libraries are predominantly open-source with no cost for academic use
2. Quantum computing services use commercial models but offer special academic access programs
3. Academic partnerships often involve substantial funding commitments ($50M+ from Google, etc.)
4. Academic access often includes additional benefits like cloud credits, training, and workforce development
